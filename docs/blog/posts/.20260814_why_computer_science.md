As an endurance athlete, I understand the reward that can come from persistent effort. Completing my first marathon in under 3 hours taught me that. I spent 6 months before the race running 60-mile weeks, oftentimes starting my run at 4:30am. Several of those months spanned the cold Boston winter. By the time I toed the start line at the LA marathon, I knew I had worked far too hard to give anything less than my best effort. By the race's end, both temps and humidity were in the 80's. I was elated after crossing the finish line in 2:57:17. 

- what running the marathon did for me
  - learned that i can still achieve difficult things
  - the feelings i experience after a big accomplishment fade quickly. what i spend months working for can happen in blink of an eye. i realized how much the process really matters. this big achievement reminded me how much hard-won achievements can boost my confidence.
  - running a fast marathon time seems less consequential than passing a class or interview. and yet, i noticed how differently i approach hobbies and career aspirations. hobbies are answering a question of: what if?. career choices and execution seems to answer a question of "what can i escape by doing this?". while i sometimes find myself in situations that are less than ideal, would i be best served by a story of escapism to motivate myself? or would i be better served by a story of curiosity and growth? is accomplishment equal to growth? how do i define accomplishments in a way that benfits me and where I want to go?

- why do i want to work with smart people?
  - pragramatically, i think it will help me become a better version of myself. i think it will help me grow more than i would have before
  - want to work in an environment where i am inspired by the people around me
    - what do i find inspiring about other people?
        - innovative, can tell stories well, "nontraditional" paths
  - what do i want to grow into?
    - how do i measure growth?
      - traditional markers exist: salary, homeownership, children, schooling, etc.

- areas to address:
  - fomo
    - fear of missing out on a better opportunity, fear of missing out on current opportunities
    - critically evaluate if something is going to help me (predict)
    - also understand that spontanaeity is important - not everything is predictable
    - how to engineer fruitful spontanaeity into my own life? that's the nature of spontanaeity I suppose - a reminder to embrace uncertainty - bravery
  - wishing things were different for me - distraction
  - energy and focus
    - how to optimize my energy for work while still staying fit/healthy?
  - "you can have it all, just not all at once" - maybe i will have to give up performance gains in cycling
    - how to form a new identity from athletic striver to academic balancer
  
College sophomore me would be incredulous if he saw what I was doing now. A masters?! In CS?! This sophomore was burnt out and in need of an escape - computer science simply was not "it". Desperately wanting to "escape poverty" or as his parents had phrased it "don't be like us", the freshman had thrust himself into the sciences against his inner yearnings for the humanities. But in early sophomore year, after too much YouTube doomscrolling (where else was a clueless lower-middle class kid from the Arizona suburbs to learn about software engineering), he decided that it would not be a worthwhile endeavor to sit in front of a computer all day in isolation for the rest of his career. I understand him very well, actually.
